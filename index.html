My teardown of xPay

I studied your product before applying, so I can get up to speed fast.

What I think xPay does, who it's for, where the friction is and what I'd look at first. It's built from your website, pricing page, launch post and job post. Please correct me where I've got it wrong.

1 · What xPay does

xPay (YC W24) is an international payment gateway for Indian businesses that sell globally. A merchant integrates once, or just sends a payment link, and can charge customers in 100+ currencies with cards, wallets and local methods. Funds settle in INR, and sales-tax and FIRC paperwork are handled for them.

2 · Who the customers are

Merchants (the businesses that pay xPay)

  • SaaS, edtech, travel, D2C and digital businesses selling abroad
  • Per the job post: B2B SaaS companies
  • Care about success rate, cost, settlement speed and paperwork

Their end customers (the people who pay)

  • International shoppers and subscribers
  • Want to pay their own way, in their own currency, without surprises
  • Never choose xPay, but feel it at the moment of payment

3 · The journey, as I understand it

1Sign upOnboard and get verified
2IntegrateAPI or no-code payment link
3Customer paysCard, wallet, BNPL or local method
4OptimiseLocal acquiring, smart retries
5SettleINR settlement, FIRC, sales tax
6ReportTransactions, risk scores, fees

4 · Pain points and how xPay answers them

5 · What I'd look at first

These come from public pages, so they're hypotheses to check against your real data.

Pricing is hard to add up

The fee list has a base rate (3% + 35¢) plus separate lines for international cards (1.5%), FX (1%), payouts (1%) and more. A founder comparing options may struggle to see their all-in cost. A "your effective rate" calculator could help.

Public numbers aren't consistent

The site gives different counts for countries (140+ and 160+) and currencies (100+, 110+ and 150+). Small things like this matter in payments, where trust is the product.

Proof is thin in public

The G2 page shows a single review. Short case studies with success-rate and conversion numbers, built from the testimonials you already have, would help buyers decide.

Settlement timing isn't on the pricing page

It's the first thing finance teams ask about. The homepage mentions 24 hours for virtual accounts, but a clear settlement promise for card payments would help.

The shopper side is mostly invisible

The 95% success claim is the biggest lever for both sides. I'd want to see decline reasons by country and card type, and what the shopper actually sees when a payment fails. A failed payment is a design problem as much as a payments one.

My 90-day plan

How I'd spend my first three months.

Learn like a customer, ship small, measure, then own an outcome.

30

Learn and earn trust

Days 1–30
  • Learn payments hands-on: put test transactions through end to end, and read decline and dispute data with the team
  • Sit in on sales calls and support (WhatsApp and phone) to find the top recurring merchant problems
  • Map two funnels: merchant onboarding (sign-up → first successful payment) and the shopper's checkout (page → paid)
  • Ship one small improvement with a spec and a clear success measure
Output: a ranked list of friction points with evidence, plus one shipped fix
60

Prove

Days 31–60
  • Write problem-first specs for the top 2–3 problems, agreed with engineering and design
  • Launch the first meaningful improvement in stages, with A/B tests where possible (e.g. clearer decline messaging, or an onboarding checklist that gets merchants to their first payment sooner)
  • Set up simple dashboards for activation and success rate
  • Run weekly merchant conversations that feed straight into the backlog
Output: one measured improvement and a first health dashboard
90

Own an outcome

Days 61–90
  • Take ownership of one metric (for example time to first successful payment, or recovery of failed payments)
  • Launch a 0→1 piece in that area and measure before and after
  • Write up what worked and what didn't
  • Propose the next quarter's roadmap
Output: a measured result on something I own, plus a roadmap proposal
